## Chapter 6 The Core Proposition of Consciousness: Existence Precedes Consciousness, Structure Precedes Agency

In this system, consciousness is not primary. We borrow the existentialist phrasing but endow it with a thoroughly materialist connotation: **existence precedes consciousness.**

We adhere to the following positions and present brief arguments for them:

1. **There is no consciousness without life**
    - **Argument**: To this day, humanity has never observed any conscious phenomenon existing independently of a physical carrier (principally biological organisms).

2. **Consciousness does not exist prior to life structures**
    - **Argument**: Developmental and evolutionary evidence indicates that in the history of Earth's evolution, simple metabolic structures came first, followed by complex nervous systems, and finally high-level cognition.

3. **Agency is the functional expression of life structures**
    - **Argument**: Physiological and pathological evidence indicates that specific structural damage (such as brain damage or genetic mutation) directly leads to the loss or alteration of specific agentic functions.

4. **The complexity of consciousness depends on structural complexity**
    - **Argument**: Comparative anatomy shows that the complexity of the nervous system (number of synapses, connectivity patterns, and compartmentalization) is positively correlated with the behavioral richness of the organism.

5. **Evolution is not teleological; it is selection and constraint**
    - **Argument**: Natural selection filters for adaptive outcomes rather than predetermining them.

To summarize briefly in one sentence:

> **Structure precedes agency;**
> **Life precedes consciousness.**
