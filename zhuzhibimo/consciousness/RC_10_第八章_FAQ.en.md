## Chapter 8 Common Misconceptions and Objections (FAQ)

### "Does aligning consciousness with agency lead to over-generalization?"

SATC adopts a working definition for origin and comparative studies, aiming to avoid using human reportability as a defining premise. Phenomenological consciousness needs to be characterized separately under higher-level structural conditions. The concern over so-called "over-generalization" usually stems from conflating two semantics: understanding "consciousness" through experiential semantics while understanding "agency" through origin semantics.

### "Is this denying subjective experience?"

No. SATC's claim is that subjective experience itself is an important object of description, but it cannot directly serve as a causal starting point in the absence of structural constraints.

### "Do plants also have consciousness?"

Under the semantics of "reportable experience," plants certainly do not possess consciousness in the human sense. Under the semantics of "minimal consciousness/primordial consciousness," plants exhibit distributed agency, forming part of a continuum. The key is not to fight over vocabulary, but to elucidate the structural conditions and variations in capacity.

### "Isn't agency just a functional label assigned by observers?"

SATC's structural functionalism requires that agency must have a traceable structural implementation, constrained by evolutionary continuity, genetic and developmental differences, as well as data on damage and pathology. Agency is not an arbitrary label, but a functional judgment constrained by structural and causal tests.

### "Is this panpsychism?"

SATC neither requires nor advocates that "everything has a mind". SATC discusses the continuum of structured, closed-loop agency in living systems.

### "Where do unconscious processing and automated behaviors fit in SATC?"

The hierarchy of SATC describes the structural conditions and capacity spectrum of agency; it does not deny the existence of a vast array of processes within the system that do not enter report or attention. On the contrary, higher levels of agency are often accompanied by more complex automation and unconscious processing. Unconscious processing is not the opposite of consciousness, but rather acts as the "chassis" of the conscious system.

### "If consciousness is agency, does consciousness equal survival instinct?"

Consciousness aligns with agency, but this does not equate to reducing everything to "instinct." Agency is a suite of structured capabilities encompassing regulation and choice across multiple timescales; it can manifest as instinctive reactions, or as learning, planning, externalization, and counterfactuals.
