## Chapter Four Evolution and Selection: The Road to the Complexity of Agency

Natural selection has no purpose.

Evolution has no direction, no endpoint, and no goal. But it presents an inescapable fact:

> **Only structures capable of surviving and reproducing in the environment are preserved.**

Here, we must guard against the **teleological trap**. We must carefully rewrite a commonly misused statement:

- ❌ "Structures possessing agency are more likely to be preserved"
- ✅ **"Those structures capable of achieving survival and inheritance in the environment—which include functions of agency—are preserved"**

The difference is: the former implies that "agency" is the goal of evolution; the latter clarifies that "agency" is merely one of the functional properties of the selected structures.

Agency is not something that is "favored"; it is simply a functional expression of living structures confronting their environment:

- Adapting to the environment
- Modifying the environment
- Evading threats
- Acquiring resources

Thus:

> **The environment selects species, and it selects agency as well.**

Agency does not exist in a vacuum; it is always attached to concrete life forms. **Evolution preserves structures, without favoring agency—yet structures carry agency.**
