More bluntly: they are worried about the **redistributive capacity of the social system**. If the wealth created by machines is intercepted by a minority of people and structures, and the majority lose their livelihoods alongside their jobs, then technological progress is no longer a blessing, but a disaster. This fear is not directed at the "replaced workers," but at the **rigidity of the system**—pointing to that systematic distribution pattern that cannot be altered by individual will.

And this is precisely the core intuition of $F\_{damage}$—the **price damage factor**—which we will discuss in this booklet.

$F\_{damage}$ measures the degree to which price deviates from equivalent exchange (I dislike the concept of equivalent exchange because exchange itself is naturally non-equivalent, but I like the non-existent assumption of rational exchange in a free market) under the joint action of intraspecific structure ($S\_s$) and tool level ($T$). When $T$ skyrockets while $S\_s$ remains unchanged, $F\_{damage}$ does not decrease but explodes—because the increment of tools is amplified into loss by the rigid structure in a power-law fashion.

**This is how the total factor productivity illusion comes about.**

Last year (2025), some economists cried out about "insufficient effective demand," and then claimed that we must wait for the development of "total factor productivity" to smooth out this "insufficient effective demand," admitting there would be a painful period in between. Fortunately, though, it is not these economists crying "painful" who suffer; they can still be re-hired after retirement, with a pension of over 8,000 yuan, keeping their original benefits unchanged, and their own household's effective demand has instead skyrocketed.

***

Therefore, when people worry about AI causing unemployment, they are essentially worrying about:

**Is our $S\_s$ (intraspecific structure) flexible enough to convert $T$ (the productivity explosion brought by AI) into an increase in $W$ (the individual's actual retention), instead of letting $W$ continue to be pressed down toward $L$ (the survival red line)?**

The newly revised **dynamic function formula for $F\_{damage}$**

$$F\_{damage} = \frac{S\_s \cdot T^{\alpha}}{1 - \beta \cdot e^{-\gamma(L - W)}}$$

If the system is rigid and distribution is ossified, then the more powerful AI becomes, the higher $F\_{damage}$ will be, the more painful it will be for those at the bottom, and the more unstable society will become. This is not the fault of technology; it is the **fault of the structure**.

**Less work, more recreation, or even not working at all—this is not degradation; it is the ultimate home of civilization.** But the premise is: we must first bring down $F\_{damage}$ to make the structure worthy of the tools.
